Frankenstein

Written by Mary Shelley

About the Book

Frankenstein is a classic Gothic novel by Mary Shelley. It follows Victor Frankenstein, a young scientist who becomes fascinated with discovering the secret of life and creates a living being through his experiments.

Detailed Summary

The story begins with Robert Walton, an explorer traveling through the Arctic. During his journey, he encounters Victor Frankenstein, who is exhausted and pursuing a mysterious creature. Victor begins telling Walton the story of his life and the events that led him to this dangerous pursuit.

Victor becomes deeply interested in science and the mysteries of life. While studying, he discovers a way to bring a lifeless body to life. Excited by his discovery, Victor creates a living being, but when the creature awakens, Victor becomes frightened by what he has made and abandons it.

The creature is left alone and must learn how to survive. He observes people and gradually learns language, emotions, and human behavior. Although he hopes to be accepted, people are often frightened by his appearance and reject him.

The creature eventually confronts Victor and explains how loneliness and rejection have affected him. He asks Victor to create a companion so that he will no longer be completely alone.

Victor struggles with the consequences of his creation. As the conflict between Victor and the creature grows, the story explores responsibility, loneliness, ambition, revenge, and the consequences of scientific discovery.

Main Themes

  • Responsibility for one's actions
  • Ambition and scientific discovery
  • Loneliness and rejection
  • Friendship and companionship
  • Revenge
  • The consequences of unchecked ambition

What You Can Learn

Frankenstein encourages readers to think about the responsibilities that come with knowledge and discovery. It also explores how loneliness, rejection, and a lack of understanding can affect individuals and their relationships with others.

About the Author

Mary Shelley

Mary Shelley was an English novelist best known for Frankenstein. Her work became an important part of Gothic literature and helped establish ideas that later became associated with science fiction.
