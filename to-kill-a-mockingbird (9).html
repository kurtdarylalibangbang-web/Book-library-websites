FICTION

To Kill a Mockingbird

Written by Harper Lee

About the Book

Scout Finch grows up in a small Alabama town and learns difficult lessons about prejudice, justice, courage, and empathy.

Detailed Summary

Scout and her brother Jem are raised by their father Atticus Finch, a lawyer who teaches them to treat people fairly. When Atticus defends Tom Robinson, the children witness how racial prejudice can influence a community. Their experiences also change how they understand their mysterious neighbor Boo Radley. The story explores justice, compassion, morality, and growing up.

What Makes This Book Interesting?

This title gives readers a chance to explore its central ideas through memorable people, events, and questions. It can also be used as a starting point for class discussion and personal reflection.

Main Themes

  • Justice
  • Prejudice
  • Empathy
  • Courage

What You Can Learn

Readers can use the book to think more deeply about its themes, compare them with real-life experiences, and form their own informed opinions.
